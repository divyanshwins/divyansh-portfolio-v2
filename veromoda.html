Performance investigation · 2026

Why Vero Moda was losing users before they even arrived

In brief

A deep-dive into Bestseller's product page performance, using GA4 funnel analysis and Lighthouse diagnostics to trace 600k+ sessions and find where — and why — users were vanishing.

CompanyBestseller / Vero Moda
ToolsGA4 · Lighthouse · PSI
Sessions analysed634,884
RoleUX + performance research

01 — Background

The company, and the business problem

Bestseller is a Danish fashion company behind global brands including Vero Moda, Jack & Jones, Only and Selected. Their growth is fuelled by performance marketing campaigns that send users directly to product pages — making page speed a revenue-critical metric.

Performance marketing spend was not converting at expected rates. Users arriving from paid ads were exiting immediately, before any meaningful interaction. The hypothesis: the drop was a performance issue, not a targeting issue.

02 — Funnel analysis

Where do users drop?

634,884 sessions were traced through the purchase funnel in GA4. The results revealed a catastrophic drop immediately after landing.

89% of users exited between the product page and Add to Cart — the single largest drop-off in the entire journey.

03 — User flow investigation

Two dominant entry journeys

User flow analysis showed that almost no traffic entered via the homepage. Ad campaigns were sending users directly into product discovery surfaces — two patterns emerged.

Flow 01 — direct to PDP

Ad click → product page → exit. Users arriving directly on the product page frequently exited without scrolling or interacting. No dwell time was recorded.

Flow 02 — product listing

Ad click → PLP → exit. Users who landed on the listing page left without discovering relevant products. Poor recommendation surfaces accelerated the drop.

Two root causes identified. Ad→PDP drop: slow product page loading causing abandonment before engagement. Ad→PLP drop: ineffective product discovery due to poor recommendation and filtering systems.

04 — Critical discovery

The bottleneck was the page itself

Deeper investigation confirmed the largest exit cluster was happening during page load — before users could see any content at all. This pointed to a performance bottleneck, not a campaign targeting problem.

05 — PageSpeed Insights

Performance diagnostics

The product page was tested using Lighthouse under Slow 4G on an emulated Moto G Power. A score of 38/100 placed it firmly in the "Poor" bracket. With LCP at 8 seconds, the page was effectively invisible by the time it rendered — for mobile users arriving from paid ads, a blank screen that never resolved.

Large network payload

Critical request chains

Dependency chains between requests introduced another 1.6 seconds of latency.

06 — UX engineering

Interface decisions driving the bloat

Observation 01 — image navigation rendering

Every product page dynamically generated one DOM dot indicator per product image. A product with 20 images created 20 dots, 20 click handlers and 20 layout recalculations — multiplied across hundreds of products viewed per session. Fix: replace dot navigation with in-frame arrow controls, dramatically reducing DOM complexity.

Observation 02 — high-resolution product images

Fashion e-commerce demands image quality — fabric texture, colour accuracy and garment detail are purchasing signals. As a result images were uploaded at maximum resolution without format optimisation, accounting for over 2 MB of avoidable payload. Reducing resolution wasn't feasible, so the fix was format: convert to WebP, holding visual quality while cutting file size by roughly 60% (~800 KB → ~300 KB).

07 — Project resolution

What was proposed and built

With the bottlenecks mapped, a two-pronged solution was designed: reduce time-to-interactive for users arriving from paid ads, and expand the product discovery surface to catch non-converting visitors.

Lazy loading implementation

Images and non-critical assets deferred until they enter the viewport — critical for the first 3-second window where abandonment is highest.

Expanded product discovery

Users who landed but didn't convert were shown additional products inline — routed into browse mode instead of exiting.

The recommendation layout moved from a 3-column carousel (only one product visible per row on mobile; users rarely scrolled) to a 2-column vertical grid with progressive loading — more visible product surface, simpler scroll interaction.

By mapping the drop-off to a specific performance bottleneck rather than a marketing problem, the investigation reframed the solution entirely. The redesigned experience reduces friction in the critical first seconds and creates secondary conversion opportunities for users who don't immediately purchase — maximising the value of every ad click.
